## Interview synthesis
Use participant codes (INT-01 and INT-02), dates, relevant circumstances, and approximately 15 minutes per interview. Ask about a specific recent experience. Summarize confirmed, contradicted, or uncertain findings; do not manufacture surprises. Keep sensitive raw notes out of the repository.

### INT-01

INT-01, college student living off-campus with roommates, streams primarily via shared/family accounts, 9/10/26, 12 min.

What happened and the participant's workaround:

Reported that when choosing what to watch, she picked a new show she'd been anticipating, but when nothing appeals, her default fallback (reported as happening ~95% of the time) is rewatching Gilmore Girls, a show she's seen 20+ times, because searching for something new is "difficult and often disappointing." She shares a Netflix account with roommates and an HBO Max account with her parents; the workaround for the shared Netflix is tolerating ads on her roommate's cheaper tier, and for HBO Max, repeatedly re-logging-in after being kicked off for being out of state.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer:

Reported: She wants to add HBO Max back, but specifically and only to watch Friends, not for other content.
Reported: Doesn't find any of her subscriptions "useless", each provides some unique value even if used rarely.
Reported: Five years ago, cable/live TV meant fewer options ("go to the kids section, pick the best of three or four"), which she says made choosing easier, not harder.
Observed: When asked to estimate her number of subscriptions before checking, she guessed 5; the actual count was 7, a two-service gap between perceived and actual spend.
Inferred: The gap between guess and reality suggests subscription costs may be less salient to her month-to-month than the friction of choosing what to watch.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain:

Confirms a framing that "more choice increases decision friction" (explicit comparison to fewer cable options being easier). Challenges any assumption that people default to canceling unused services, she keeps all 7 despite an unprompted 2-service undercount and describes each as valuable. Leaves uncertain whether the Gilmore Girls fallback is really about content fatigue or about avoiding the search/decision process itself; she describes it as the latter, but this is self-report from one respondent.


### INT-02

INT-02, fraternity member with primarily personal (non-shared) subscriptions, occasional use of a shared house account, 9/10/26, 20 min.

What happened and the participant's workaround:

Reported that content choice is driven by recommendation, educational value, or visual/conceptual interest. When he can't find something within his subscriptions, his workaround is switching platforms entirely, going to YouTube for old videos,  rather than searching harder within a streaming app, or in some cases pirating content or waiting for it to appear on a subscription he already has.

What was reported, what you observed, and what you infer:

Reported: His viewing habits shifted from "watching whatever was on, out of order, as a kid" to now "sitting down and watching with intent" as an adult.
Reported: Has used a shared fraternity house account in common areas but has never shared a personal account.
Reported: Described a specific decision flow: scroll apps → pick based on time available and short vs. long form preference → if long-form, check "Recommended" then "My List" → if nothing there, scroll until something interesting appears.
Observed: Estimated 4 subscriptions before checking; actual count was 5, a smaller but same direction gap as INT-01 (underestimating spend by one service).
Inferred: His fallback to piracy or waiting, rather than paying for a new subscription, suggests a higher price resistance threshold than INT-01, who is actively looking to add one back.

Evidence that confirms, challenges, or leaves the framing uncertain:
Confirms a cross-participant pattern (subscription undercounting) seen in INT-01, suggesting the "hidden subscription creep" effect may not be a one-off. Challenges a framing centered on decision fatigue driving rewatch behavior, INT-02's fallback when stuck is switching platforms (YouTube) or waiting/pirating, not defaulting to a rewatch. Leaves uncertain whether "watching with intent" is really a maturity/age shift or a product of streaming specifically (he attributes it to no longer needing to "catch" live-airing shows, which is at least partly a platform effect, not just personal growth).

## Two job statements
JOB-01: When I can't decide what to watch and searching feels overwhelming, I want to have a safe option I already trust, so I can start watching without the risk of picking something disappointing. Evidence: INT-01's near automatic Gilmore Girls fallback ("99% of the time"), explicitly framed as avoiding disappointment rather than genuine preference for that show over anything new.
JOB-02: When I sit down to watch something, I want to watch with focus and in order, so I can actually follow and appreciate what I'm watching (rather than half-watching whatever's on). Evidence: his explicit before/after comparison of childhood casual watching vs. now watching "with intent."

## Two user profiles

Role: College student, lives off-campus with roommates; shares streaming accounts with roommates and family rather than paying individually for most services.
Circumstances: Has access to multiple streaming platforms but frequently can't decide what to watch; defaults to rewatching a familiar comfort show (Gilmore Girls, 20+ times) roughly 99% of the time when nothing new appeals (reported).
Needs: A low-effort, low-risk way to land on something to watch without the disappointment risk of trying something new (reported job of avoiding a bad pick, not genuinely preferring the rewatch).
Constraints: Budget conscious as a student; relies on shared accounts, which introduces its own friction (ads on roommate's cheaper Netflix tier, repeated logouts from HBO Max due to out-of-state location) (reported).
Evidence IDs: INT-01, JOB-01

PROFILE-02:

Role: Fraternity member with primarily personal (non-shared) subscriptions; occasional access to a shared house account in common areas.
Circumstances: Watches with more deliberate intent than in the past, as a kid watched whatever aired, out of order, now sits down and watches purposefully (reported). Follows a structured decision path: scrolls apps → picks based on time available and short vs. long form preference → checks "Recommended" and "My List" → browses if nothing found there (reported).
Needs: A way to watch with focus and in the right order/context, so he can actually follow and appreciate what he's watching, rather than consuming content passively or out of sequence (reported explicit contrast between "didn't care to watch things in order" as a kid versus "sitting down and watching with intent" now).
Constraints: Higher price-resistance than PROFILE-01 when his existing subscriptions don't have what he wants, his fallback is switching to YouTube, waiting, or pirating rather than adding a new subscription (reported).
Evidence IDs: INT-02, JOB-02
